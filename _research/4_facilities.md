  <div class="research-detail-top__text">
    <p>
      The lab operates out of a dedicated high-bay space at 2420 Alamo Ave SE in Albuquerque,
      a few minutes from UNM's main campus. It is built around the world's first academic
      rotating detonation engine materials testbed: a facility whose purpose is not to optimize
      an engine, but to put candidate wall materials into a real detonation environment and
      measure what happens to them.
    </p>
    <p>
      Capabilities include:
      <ul>
        <li><ins>Rotating detonation engine testbed:</ins> Hydrogen–air operation with instrumented, swappable wall sections for materials evaluation.</li>
        <li><ins>Thermal instrumentation:</ins> Calorimetric heat flux measurement, embedded thermocouples, and surface temperature diagnostics for high-rate transient loading.</li>
        <li><ins>Data acquisition:</ins> High-rate acquisition and analysis of pressure, temperature, and heat flux traces across a firing.</li>
        <li><ins>Sample preparation and post-test analysis:</ins> Evaluation of coated and multilayered specimens before and after exposure.</li>
      </ul>
    </p>
  </div>
