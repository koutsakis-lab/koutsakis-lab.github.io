  <div class="research-detail-top__text">
    <p>
      Conduction, convection, and radiation set the limits on how hard a propulsion or energy
      system can be pushed. We develop analytical, numerical, and experimental methods for
      thermal transport in the walls of those systems — especially walls built from several
      dissimilar layers, where the interfaces govern the answer.
    </p>
    <p>
      Our work in this area includes:
      <ul>
        <li><ins>Unsteady multilayer conduction:</ins> Closed-form and semi-analytical solutions for surface temperature and heat flux in coated engine walls subject to rapidly varying loads.</li>
        <li><ins>Inverse heat transfer:</ins> Recovering surface conditions from interior measurements, including partial singular value decomposition approaches for multilayer walls.</li>
        <li><ins>Convective cooling:</ins> Flow and heat transfer characteristics of additively manufactured mini channels for compact, high-flux cooling.</li>
        <li><ins>Thermal radiation:</ins> Radiative transport in semi-transparent ceramic systems and highly reflective fibrous insulation.</li>
      </ul>
    </p>

    <h3>Multilayer Wall Analysis</h3>
    <p>
      Analytical solutions for instantaneous surface temperature in multilayer coated walls give
      engine designers a fast, physically transparent alternative to full conjugate heat transfer
      simulation.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="research-detail-card">
    <div class="figure-placeholder">Figure coming soon</div>
    <h3>Additively Manufactured Cooling</h3>
    <p>
      Additive manufacturing allows cooling channels that conventional machining cannot produce.
      We characterize their flow and heat transfer behavior, including the roughness and geometry
      effects the printing process introduces.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="research-detail-card">
    <div class="figure-placeholder">Figure coming soon</div>
    <h3>Radiation &amp; Insulation</h3>
    <p>
      Semi-transparent ceramics do not simply block heat — they absorb, scatter, and re-emit it.
      We model that behavior and use it to design highly reflective fibrous insulation and
      radiation barrier coatings.
    </p>
