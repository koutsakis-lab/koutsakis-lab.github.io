  <div class="research-detail-top__text">
    <p>
      A thermal barrier coating is asked to insulate and to survive, and the two goals fight each
      other. Thicker and less conductive means a hotter surface, a steeper gradient, and more
      stored elastic energy available to drive a crack. A coating that insulates beautifully for
      one firing and spalls on the hundredth has not solved the problem.
    </p>
    <p>
      We treat durability as a fracture problem rather than a thermal one. Transient conduction
      gives the temperature field, thermoelasticity gives the stress, and an energy release rate
      compared against the measured toughness says whether and where a delamination will run.
    </p>
    <p>Our work in this area includes:</p>
    <ul>
      <li><ins>Fracture-based durability analysis:</ins> Time-resolved energy release rate through the coating thickness, predicting the depth at which delamination initiates rather than only whether the coating insulates.</li>
      <li><ins>Delamination in real engines:</ins> Model predictions checked against coatings run to failure on high-output reciprocating engines.</li>
      <li><ins>Architecture optimization:</ins> Ranking coating thickness and property combinations on performance and durability together, over a full drive cycle rather than a single operating point.</li>
      <li><ins>Coatings under detonation:</ins> Extending the same framework to the far higher frequencies and heat fluxes of a rotating detonation environment.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>

    <h3>Delamination Mechanics</h3>
    <p>
      Comparing the stored elastic energy of the intact wall against the cracked configuration
      gives the driving force for delamination — the quantity that has to stay below the
      interface toughness.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="research-detail-card">
    <img src="/assets/img/research/coating_stress_err.png" alt="Stress and energy release rate versus crank angle at four depths through the coating, from the surface to the interface">
    <h3>Through the Cycle</h3>
    <p>
      The surface goes into deep compression at combustion while the interface barely moves. The
      crack driving force peaks well after the heat flux does, and it grows with depth.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="research-detail-card">
    <img src="/assets/img/research/coating_tradeoff.png" alt="Net in-cylinder heat transfer reduction and the ratio of maximum energy release rate to toughness, both plotted against coating thickness, marking the best performance point and the cracking limit">
    <h3>Performance Against Life</h3>
    <p>
      Thicker coatings insulate better right up until they crack. Plotting both curves together
      puts the useful design window between them, rather than at either extreme.
    </p>
